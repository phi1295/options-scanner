Setup Guide
One-time setup and weekly IBD50 import. For trading steps, see the Daily Playbook.
One-time setup
1

Install Python packages

pip install flask schwab-py pandas numpy requests lxml

The lxml package is required for fetching the S&P 500 and Nasdaq 100 universe from Wikipedia.

2

Edit config.json

Open config.json and replace the placeholders with your Schwab Client ID and Client Secret from the Schwab Developer Portal. This file is never overwritten by updates.

3

First launch — Schwab authentication

Run python app.py from PyCharm (use the Run button, not Terminal, to ensure the virtual environment is active). The PyCharm console will print a Schwab login URL and show these instructions:

1. A URL will appear below 2. Copy it and open it in any browser 3. Log in to Schwab and click Approve 4. Copy the FULL URL from the browser bar 5. Paste it here in the console and press Enter

Token saves for 7 days. Re-authenticate weekly by deleting schwab_token.json and restarting.

Callback URL in the Schwab Developer Portal must be exactly https://127.0.0.1:8182 — no trailing slash.
IBD50 import (weekly, every Monday)
4

Export from ibd.com

Go to ibd.com → IBD50 → click the Export/Download button to get the XLS file. Drag it onto the IBD50 Import tab. All 24 columns are parsed automatically — RS Rating, EPS Rating, Composite, ACC/DIS, % off High, earnings growth, and more. These feed directly into the setup score.

5

What the IBD data does

  • RS Rating — uses IBD's actual rating instead of a price-based proxy
  • ACC/DIS D or E — stock automatically excluded even if RS is high
  • % off High >35% — excluded in bullish regime (stock is in a downtrend)
  • IBD Rank 1–10 — gets highest score bonus vs rank 40–50
  • Composite + EPS — both contribute to the setup score
  • IBD50 stocks screened first — always appear at the top of scan results
Daily Playbook
Entering trades, setting exit orders, and the daily routine. Step numbers continue from the Setup Guide.
Entering trades in thinkorswim
6

Enter as a single vertical spread

  • Trade tab → type the ticker → open the Options Chain
  • Find the expiration date shown on the trade card
  • Click the Ask on your buy strike (e.g. $525)
  • Hold Ctrl and click the Bid on your sell strike (e.g. $535)
  • Order ticket must say BUY +1 VERTICAL — not SELL
  • Set as Limit order at the net debit shown on the card
Always verify the order says BUY VERTICAL before sending. If it says SELL, click the Buy/Sell toggle to flip it.
7

Set your profit target after entry

  • After your spread fills, right-click the position in Monitor → Activity and Positions
  • Select Create Closing Order → Sell the vertical to close
  • Set price to your profit target from the trade card
  • Set order type to Limit GTC (Good Till Cancelled)
  • Do NOT create a second order for your stop loss — manage the stop manually
Entering iron condors in thinkorswim
8

When the market is neutral — four-leg condor

In a neutral regime the scanner builds iron condors instead of verticals. A condor has four legs: two you SELL (collect premium) and two you BUY (protection). The trade card shows each leg clearly labeled.

  • Trade tab → type the ticker → open the Options Chain
  • Find the expiration shown on the card
  • Sell the inner call — click the Bid on the short call strike (closer to price)
  • Buy the outer call — Ctrl+click the Ask on the long call strike (further out)
  • Sell the inner put — Ctrl+click the Bid on the short put strike
  • Buy the outer put — Ctrl+click the Ask on the long put strike
  • TOS recognizes all four legs as an IRON CONDOR order
  • The order shows a net credit (money you receive) — set as Limit at the credit shown on the card
A condor order should show a CREDIT (positive number you collect), not a debit. The two SELL strikes are the inner ones closer to the current price; the two BUY strikes are your outer protection.
9

Close the condor — opposite direction

Closing a condor is the reverse of a vertical. You collected a credit at entry, so you close by paying to buy it back — ideally cheaper than you sold it.

  • Right-click the condor position in Monitor → Activity and Positions
  • Select Create Closing Order → Buy the iron condor to close
  • Set price to your profit target (50% of the credit collected — e.g. collected $2.00, target buy-back at $1.00)
  • Set order type to Limit GTC
Condor profit = collect high, buy back low. The Daily Review tab tells you exactly when to close and shows "BUY the iron condor to close" so you never get the direction wrong.
Strategy & position sizing
10

How the scanner picks spreads — built for win rate

The scanner buys slightly in-the-money spreads (long leg about 2-3% in the money, roughly 60-65 delta) rather than at-the-money. Being in the money at entry means the stock barely has to move for the trade to work — this raises your win rate and means a slightly-ITM spread doesn't whipsaw on every intraday move, so once-a-day checking is enough.

  • Return target is 25-50% on debit — clears your 25% minimum while keeping spreads in high-probability territory
  • Max loss is always the debit you pay — defined and capped, no matter what the stock does
  • Higher win rate, smaller swings, lower stress than chasing 100%+ at-the-money spreads
11

Set your account size and risk — the Position Sizing panel

At the top of the Scanner page, set two numbers:

  • Account size — your real balance. Start at $10,000 and update it as your account grows. This is the one number linking the tool to your actual account, so keep it current.
  • Risk per trade — 1.5% is the recommended default. On a $10k account that's $150 of risk per trade.

Every trade card then shows a recommended contract count sized so each trade risks about that same dollar amount at your stop loss. At $10k you'll usually see 1-2 contracts — that's correct and deliberately conservative. An amber warning appears only if a single contract would risk more than 3% of your account.

Position sizing is the single most important thing protecting your account. The recommended size keeps any one loss small and consistent — the opposite of putting 18 contracts on one trade.
Daily workflow
12

Run the scanner 10am–3:30pm ET

Check that your account size is current in the Position Sizing panel, then scan. Options liquidity peaks mid-session. Use the recommended contract count on each card — don't oversize. If the regime shows neutral (the scanner builds iron condors), consider sitting out and waiting for a bullish regime with clean bull call setups.

13

When to execute — timing matters

Enter trades in the same sitting as the scan, but avoid the first and last 30 minutes of the day:

  • Best window: 10:00 AM – 3:00 PM ET. The open (9:30–10:00) and close (3:30–4:00) have the widest bid/ask spreads and worst fills.
  • Execute within ~15-30 minutes of scanning. The card prices off the live chain at scan time. Wait too long and the stock moves, so the debit you saw may no longer be available.
  • Verify the price before sending. In thinkorswim, check the net debit matches the card within about $0.10-0.15. If it drifted more, adjust your limit to the current mid or re-scan.
  • Always use a Limit order, never Market. Set the limit at the card's debit. If it doesn't fill in a few minutes, nudge up $0.05 at a time.
  • You don't have to take every setup. 2-3 good positions at a time is plenty at a $10k account.
14

Set your profit target order — immediately after the fill

As soon as your spread fills, set ONE closing order at your profit target. Don't wait — set it the same minute you're filled, so the trade can close itself while you're not watching.

  • Right-click the position → Create Closing Order → Sell the vertical to close
  • Set the price to the Profit target shown on the trade card (50% of max profit)
  • Order type Limit, duration GTC (Good Till Cancelled)
  • Set the quantity to your full contract count
  • For an iron condor instead: Buy the iron condor to close at 50% of the credit collected
Set only ONE order — the profit target. Do NOT also place a separate stop-loss order at the same time. Two simultaneous closing orders on one position can leave you with a naked short if one fills and you forget to cancel the other.
15

Handle the stop loss manually — don't pre-place it

thinkorswim doesn't support reliable stop orders on spreads, and placing a second closing order alongside your profit target is risky. Instead, manage the stop by checking the Daily Review:

  • The Daily Review tab flags CLOSE — STOP HIT when the spread drops to your stop (50% of debit)
  • When you see that flag: first cancel your working profit-target order, then create a new closing order at market or a limit near the current bid
  • Cancelling the profit target FIRST is what prevents the naked-short situation
  • Optional: set a price alert in TOS on the underlying stock near your stop level as a reminder to check
16

Morning and midday — check Daily Review

Open the Daily Review tab and click Refresh. It fetches live spread prices from Schwab for all your open positions and tells you exactly what to do with each one. Actions include:

  • CLOSE — TARGET HIT — sell vertical (or buy condor) to close
  • CLOSE — STOP HIT — cancel profit target, close at market
  • CLOSE — 21 DTE RULE — time decay rule, close regardless of P&L
  • WATCH — APPROACHING STOP — monitor closely
  • HOLD — between levels, nothing to do
17

After closing a trade — update your account size

When you close a position, update the Account size field to your new real balance from Schwab. This keeps the contract recommendations accurate as your account grows or shrinks. The app can't see your brokerage balance, so this manual step is what keeps sizing honest.

18

Exit rules — follow every time

  • Close at profit target (50% of max profit) — the Daily Review will flag this
  • Close at stop loss (50% of debit paid) — no exceptions, no hoping for recovery
  • Close at 21 DTE — theta decay accelerates sharply after this point
  • Never hold through earnings — the scanner filters these but always verify
  • If the market regime flips against your position, close immediately
  • Add every trade to the Trade Log so Daily Review can track it
